## Approaches considered

1. **Direct row join on ZIP:** rejected. Projects and crashes have no shared event key. Many-to-many joins multiply counts and cannot establish an individual building/crash relationship.
2. **ZIP × year as the sole key:** retains Member 1's granularity but has serious coverage bias: usable SIRTA ZIP coverage is 16.26% in 2022, 68.06% in 2023, 69.93% in 2024 and 79.27% in 2025 after cleaning. Missing geography is not a zero-crash neighborhood.
3. **Borough × year primary, ZIP × year secondary:** selected. Five boroughs have much more complete geography and yield 20 matched full-year cells (2022–2025). The spatial scale is coarse and statistical power is limited, so the small-area check is retained with its selection limits.

## Cleaning policies

- Read API geographic/record identifiers as strings; trim and normalize whitespace; standardize borough names to five uppercase labels; preserve IDs without numeric decimals. ZIP accepts a whole five-digit code or ZIP+4 (and numeric `.0` formatting). A broad NYC numeric range is only a domain check, not proof of a postal boundary. ZIPs observed under multiple borough labels are excluded from the secondary research chart rather than forced into a borough. No external crosswalk or third dataset is introduced.
- Parse dates with coercion. Quarantine invalid/missing crash dates and conflicting collision IDs; exclude their values from aggregates. Three exact duplicates are removed. Of the remaining rows, 441 lack dates and two share a conflicting collision ID (4597666); all 443 are quarantined. This leaves 13,308 usable dated SIRTA records; 55 lack a usable borough and 5,280 lack a usable ZIP. Missing borough/ZIP records stay in the clean record table and are omitted only from the affected geographic aggregate.
- Retain all 9,551 housing records. No exact duplicates or conflicting complete project/building keys are found. Missing ZIP affects 1,958 records but does not remove their valid borough information. Five completion-before-start cases are flagged and retained; project-start year remains usable and no duration conclusion is drawn.
- Unit/injury counts must be nonnegative integers; invalid values become unknown and are audited, not filled with zero. The frozen data have no invalid observed count values. Twenty-nine housing records lack counted-unit values; two fall in the main 2022–2025 window. Missing optional income/bedroom details stay unknown. Missing completion dates are allowed for ongoing work.
- IQR flags mark 1,171 high-unit housing records and 958 unusual SIRTA severity counts. SIRTA's severity IQR is zero (Q1=Q3=1), so the flag includes valid counts below/above one. These are reviewed/domain-plausible records and retained, not winsorized away; rare multi-injury events matter to safety. Numeric EDA and flagged clean rows expose their values. Invalid coordinates, if present, become unknown; location is not guessed from addresses.
- Strict aggregate unit totals are NA if any contributing unit value is unknown. `observed_counted_units` separately sums known records with `min_count=1`; it is explicitly a known subtotal. M2 Q1 uses those known subtotals and discloses the two unknown records. M2 Q2 and M3 Q2 use complete-value cells only. M3 Q1 shows gaps for the two incomplete borough-years rather than connecting fabricated totals.

## Aggregation and post-integration policies

Housing: group by borough/start year or ZIP/start year; count records, distinct building IDs, sum strict total/countable units, record missing counts, and derive new-construction share. Transport: group by borough/crash year or ZIP/crash year; count records and sum known severe injuries/fatalities. A per-crash severity measure divides recorded severely/fatally injured people by SIRTA records; it can exceed one and is not a percentage or per-capita risk.

Both inputs must have unique, non-null aggregate keys before the outer join. `validate='one_to_one'` rejects multiplicative joins. Align year to nullable integer, ZIP and borough to text. Retain `join_status`; housing-only/transport-only measures stay NA. Historic housing-only years (2014–2021) are outside SIRTA coverage and are not interpreted as safe years. There are 25 matched borough/year rows (2022–2026), 40 housing-only historic rows, and no transport-only borough/year rows. The primary chart window excludes 2026. After join, consolidate geographic columns, reject borough mismatches in secondary analysis, enforce metric dtypes and keep explicit eligibility flags. ZIP outer join: 484 matched, 671 housing-only and 340 transport-only rows across all years; 308 eligible secondary rows, of which 306 have complete strict housing counts for Q2. Three ambiguous ZIP labels (10463, 11237, 11385) are excluded from that research analysis.

## Interpretation limits and practical use

HPD counted units include preservation and new construction associated with project starts. They do not describe every newly built unit, all housing stock, occupancy or resident exposure. SIRTA contains investigated serious crashes and can be affected by investigation/reporting selection and coverage changes. Borough totals hide street-level differences. ZIP completeness changes by year and source; no imputed ZIP counts are used. Correlations pool repeated boroughs/ZIPs over years, are descriptive, unadjusted, and have no independent-observation or causal interpretation. Staten Island's 2022 housing baseline is just 33 counted units; its large indexed growth is a small-denominator effect as well as project mix and must not be called proof of a housing boom.

Use these findings to prioritize coordinated HPD/DOT review and further local data collection. They do not identify individual developments as dangerous or justify allocating funds solely from these counts.
